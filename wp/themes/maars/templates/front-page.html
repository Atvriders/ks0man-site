On the air

The KSØMAN repeater

  • Output 147.255 MHz
  • Input 147.855 MHz, a +600 kHz offset
  • Tone CTCSS 88.5 Hz, transmit and receive
  • Access open to any licensed amateur

Nobody has re-measured any of it since the machine left the KSDB-FM tower in November 2023 and the controller was voted out of the system in January 2024. The spectrum band across the top of every page is a drawing of this frequency, not a receiver listening to it.

The Tuesday net

A directed net runs on the repeater every Tuesday at 8:00 P.M. Central, and any amateur, Technician and above, is welcome to check in. It ran at 9:00 P.M. through 2014 and appears at 8:00 P.M. from about 2016; the old site printed both hours, in different places, for years.

Where the signal goes

Drag to orbit the scene, then change bands. On 80, 40 and 20 metres the ionosphere bends the wave back to earth hundreds of miles away; on 2 metres it does not, which is why the KSØMAN repeater sits on a hill.

Amateur radio in Manhattan, Kansas since 1976

The Manhattan Area Amateur Radio Society was formed on 7 July 1976, at a founding meeting called to order at 7:40 P.M. by Leroy Buller, KØWA. Before the founders chose a name they chose the noun: they voted to be a Society rather than a club, and the S in MAARS has been deliberate ever since.

We hold the club callsign KSØMAN, we keep the repeater open to any licensed amateur, and we train and turn out storm spotters for Riley County. Under Article VII a quorum is the members present, so the meeting you turn up to is the meeting that decides things.
